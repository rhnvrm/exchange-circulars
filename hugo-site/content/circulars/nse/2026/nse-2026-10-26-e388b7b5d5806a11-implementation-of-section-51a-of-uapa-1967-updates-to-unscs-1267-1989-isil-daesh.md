## Summary

NSE has issued a circular regarding updates to the United Nations Security Council's (UNSC) 1267/1989 ISIL (Da'esh) and Al-Qaida Sanctions List, pursuant to Section 51A of the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967 (UAPA). The updates reflect amendments to 03 entries on the sanctions list based on UNSC Note SC/16480 dated October 15, 2026.

## Key Points

- UNSC Committee established pursuant to resolutions 1267 (1999), 1989 (2011) and 2253 (2015) amended 03 entries on its ISIL and Al-Qaida Sanctions List.
- Stock exchanges, depositories, KRAs, and registered intermediaries must ensure strict compliance with Section 51A of UAPA, 1967.
- Intermediaries must verify that they do not maintain any accounts or provide financial services to individuals or entities appearing on the updated sanctions lists.
- Delisting requests by regulated entities must be forwarded electronically to Joint Secretary (CTCR), MHA with a copy to Joint Secretary (UNP), MEA.

## Regulatory Changes

- Incorporation of updated UNSC ISIL (Da'esh) and Al-Qaida Sanctions List entries (Note SC/16480 dated October 15, 2026) under SEBI Master Circular on AML/CFT standards dated June 06, 2024.
- Continued enforcement of asset freezing, travel bans, and arms embargoes mandated by UN Security Council resolutions and Central Government Order F. No. 14014/01/2019/CFT.

## Compliance Requirements

- Stock Exchanges, Depositories, KRAs, and registered intermediaries must immediately screen their database of clients and accounts against the updated UNSC consolidated and Sanctions lists.
- Ensure immediate freezing of assets/accounts and denial of financial services if any matching individual or entity is identified.
- Forward any delisting requests electronically to the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) at jsctcr-mha@gov.in and Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) at jsunp@mea.gov.in.

## Important Dates

- Circular Date: October 22, 2026
- UNSC Note Date: October 15, 2026
- SEBI Master Circular Reference: June 06, 2024

## Impact Assessment

- Operational impact is moderate, requiring automated database screening against updated UNSC sanctions lists.
- High importance on legal and regulatory compliance to prevent terrorist financing and adhere to AML/CFT standards under PMLA and UAPA.
