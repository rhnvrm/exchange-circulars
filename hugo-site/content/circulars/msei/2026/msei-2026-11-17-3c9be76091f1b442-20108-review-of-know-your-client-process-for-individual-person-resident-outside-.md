## Summary

In order to ease the on-boarding process of individual Persons Resident Outside India (PROIs)—including Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs), and foreign nationals—SEBI and MSEI have reviewed the existing KYC framework. The revised guidelines relax the physical location requirement in India during digital on-boarding for clients located in Financial Action Task Force (FATF) compliant countries.

## Key Points

- **Relaxation of Physical Presence**: Individual PROI clients located in FATF-compliant countries can now complete digital on-boarding without requiring physical presence in India.
- **Attribute-Level KRA Validation**: KRAs will tag individual attributes as "validated" if verified against official source databases, while ensuring all KYC records remain portable across intermediaries.
- **Reliance on Other Regulators**: Intermediaries may rely on KYC performed by other SEBI-registered intermediaries or entities regulated by other financial sector regulators via KRA/CKYCRR records.
- **Video In-Person Verification (VIPV)**: Authorised officials of intermediaries must be present during VIPV, which is subject to concurrent audit.

## Regulatory Changes

- Modification of the extant provisions of the KYC Master Circular to permit digital submission of KYC information and supporting documents from PROI clients in FATF-compliant jurisdictions.
- Streamlining KRA record-sharing and attribute validation processes.

## Compliance Requirements

- Intermediaries must ensure that PROI clients are located in FATF-compliant countries when accepting digital KYC submissions.
- Ensure authorised officials participate in VIPV sessions and maintain concurrent audit records.
- Verify client identity, purpose, and nature of business relationship at account opening.

## Important Dates

- **Circular Date**: November 04, 2026

## Impact Assessment

This review significantly reduces administrative friction and onboarding time for non-resident retail and institutional investors looking to access Indian capital markets, thereby boosting foreign participation while upholding robust AML/CFT standards.
