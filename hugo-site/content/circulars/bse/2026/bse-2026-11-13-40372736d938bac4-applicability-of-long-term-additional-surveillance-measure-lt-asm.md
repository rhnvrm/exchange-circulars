## Summary

BSE has announced the application and revision of Long Term Additional Surveillance Measures (LT-ASM) on specified securities pursuant to the revised LT-ASM framework. Effective dates for various surveillance actions range from November 16, 2026, to November 18, 2026.

## Key Points

- 100% margins applicable w.e.f. November 18, 2026, on all open positions as on November 17, 2026, and new positions created thereafter.
- Certain securities moved to higher stages of LT-ASM and/or subject to Lower Price Band w.e.f. November 16, 2026.
- Securities continuing in LT-ASM transferred to T / XT / MT / TS Group w.e.f. November 18, 2026.
- Certain securities moving out of LT-ASM framework w.e.f. November 16, 2026, with price bands reinstated.
- Consolidated list of securities attached as annexures (Annexure I Parts A, B, D, Annexure II, and Annexure III).

## Regulatory Changes

- Imposition of 100% margins on open and new positions for securities under LT-ASM Annexure I (Part A).
- Imposition of Lower Price Band and movement to higher stages of LT-ASM for securities in Annexure I (Part B).
- Transfer of specific securities to T / XT / MT / TS Group under LT-ASM Annexure I (Part D).
- Removal of securities from LT-ASM and reinstatement of original price bands.

## Compliance Requirements

- Market participants and trading members must ensure 100% margin collection for applicable securities starting November 18, 2026.
- Monitor open positions and comply with revised price bands and group transfers effective November 16 and November 18, 2026.

## Important Dates

- November 16, 2026: Movement to higher stages of LT-ASM, Lower Price Band applicability, and removal of securities from LT-ASM.
- November 17, 2026: Cut-off date for open positions subject to 100% margins.
- November 18, 2026: 100% margins effective; transfer to T / XT / MT / TS Group effective.

## Impact Assessment

Market participants trading in securities listed under the LT-ASM annexures will experience increased margin requirements, potential liquidity constraints due to lower price bands, and movement to specialized trade-for-trade groups.
