## Summary

BSE has announced the movement of specified securities into their respective higher stages of the Graded Surveillance Measure (GSM) framework, effective from November 23, 2026. Trading members must take adequate precautions as these securities attract enhanced surveillance actions and trade-to-trade settlement without netting off.

## Key Points

- Effective Date: November 23, 2026.
- Framework applies across multiple stages (Stage I, II, III, IV) with varying surveillance actions.
- Settlement for affected securities will be on a trade-to-trade basis with no netting off allowed.
- Queries and clarifications can be directed to bse.surv@bseindia.com.

## Regulatory Changes

Securities identified in Annexure_GSM are subjected to enhanced surveillance measures according to the GSM framework:
- **Stage I**: Applicable margin rate of 100% and price band of 5% or lower.
- **Stage II**: Trade-for-trade with price band of 5% or lower and Additional Surveillance Deposit (ASD) of 50% of trade value by buyers.
- **Stage III**: Trade-for-trade with price band of 5% or lower, trading permitted once a week (every Monday/1st trading day of the week), and ASD of 100% of trade value by buyers.
- **Stage IV**: Trade-for-trade with price band of 5% or lower, trading permitted once a week (every Monday/1st trading day of the week) with ASD of 100% of trade value by buyers and no upward movement.

## Compliance Requirements

- Trading members must exercise adequate caution while trading in securities listed in the Annexure.
- Ensure compliance with applicable margin requirements, ASD deposits, and frequency restrictions based on the assigned GSM stage.
- Ensure trade-to-trade settlement execution without netting off.

## Important Dates

- **Notice Date**: November 20, 2026
- **Effective Date of GSM Movement**: November 23, 2026

## Impact Assessment

Trading activity and liquidity in the affected securities will be significantly restricted due to trade-for-trade settlement, mandatory ASD requirements, and restricted trading frequencies for higher stages, minimizing speculative trading risks.
